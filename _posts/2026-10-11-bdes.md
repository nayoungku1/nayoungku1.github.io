Most of my friends ask me how I earned a bachelor’s degree in two years during my high school years, so I prepared how I did that!
> TL; DL:
> * I took the GED when I was in Grade 10.
> * Korean government offers a degree program that can be completed *theoretically* in one year by taking a series of exams, and this degree is equivalent to a 4-year undergraduate education.
> * I passed it in two years.
> * Do I recommend this to everyone? Well, it depends on who you are. Efficiency is not everything.

Before beginning, the thing that you should know why it was possible for me to do this during high school years is...
**My secondary school was not accredited by the Korean government!**

It was a SUPER prestigious & private boarding school that followed Christian and holistic education, which means this school highlighted freedom of speech and leadership training, so it refused to get accredited. However, that doesn’t mean their education was somehow strange; this school is an IB school now, but it is still not accredited by “Korean GOV” ONLY.  And many of my classmates went to universities worldwide.  (I swear, I really love my school so much!)

Anyway, that was **my chance to take the GED** while attending high school because I was not at school in the Korean government. So when I was 15, I took the GED, which means I was qualified for the next step documentally!

---

And then, my teacher came up to me and said, “As you arleady got the GED, there is a way to get a bachelor’s degree in one year!!!”

*Whhaaaaaaatttttt😮*

### How is that even possible….???

The program is called "Bachelor's Degree Examination for Self-Education System" or **BDES**(독학학위제 in Korean).

You don't have to be Korean to take this exam, but you have to have equivalency to high school graduate! (btw, this exam is all in Korean)

It was originally made for people who could not afford higher education. It consists of four levels of examinations. One exam in a quarter: 
* On Feb, it's LIBERAL-ARTS EXAM. You should pass 5 general elective subjects that are relevant to your major. 
* On May, MAJOR-BASIC EXAM round! Now you should pass 6 fundamental major subjects, equivalent to sophomore year in university. 
* On August, MAJOR-ADVANCED EXAM round! You should pass 6 advanced major subjects, equivalent to junior year in university. 
* On Oct, it's COMPREHENSIVE EXAM! To take the last exam, you have to pass all the other exams before that or equivalent to senior year in university. You should take 2 gen ed subjects and 4 advanced major subjects. And this round's score will decide your entire GPA!

As far as I know, there are similar programs in other countries as well. 
* **China**: STHEE(The Self-Taught Higher Education Examination, 高等教育自学考试)
* **USA**: CLEP (College-Level Examination Program) 


### And a **warning sign** here… 
This exam is SUPER hard. 

Not because I took them while attending classes in high school, but because I should study without any guidance. The only thing they offer is a table of contents. So only thing you gotta do is memorize literally "everything" on the textbook and listening to every sources on MOOC or K-MOOC or such public higher education sources via the internet about the 5-6 subjects in 3 months. If you first encounter the textbook, you have no idea what is important or not... and no mates studying together with me is a plus. 
Because you should memorize and make every piece of knowledge that requires one year at college in a quarter, you have to study persistently and know YOUR learning style. You can’t cooperate with others but only with yourself. You should trust that consistency makes you better day by day. 

---

But as much as you invest your time and effort, you will earn it in one day. It might take more than 4 years if you couldn't pass though.
I would not recommend this path to everyone, to be honest. It is true that this exam seems very "efficient;" however, its super lonely and stressful. But if you are super passionate about something and have a concrete goal, it might be a good option for you. 

But by going through such processes, I could earn:
* **How to acquire knowledge and make it my own from scratch**. For me, I read the textbook 3 times and make my own notes!
* **What my true interest is**. In my case, I started psychology due to curiosity about the mechanisms of unconsciousness and abnormality, and I realized my true passion comes from curiosity about human biology! (Thanks to the exam on cognitive neuroscience!) 
* And of course, **a degree**. But I should explain to everyone how I earned it at 18 years old in one sentence.

### Do I recommend this path?

The best thing is that I saved time by just diving into the studies that I was interested in. If I didn’t study psychology at that time, I think I would never have known I actually loved discovering the mechanisms of disease and creating a new method, not just observing the behavior.

Even though you got bachelor's degree through this program, I don't think it makes you ready for graduate program right after the degree conferral. I am not an admission committee, but I don't think it's enough. Because those knowledges are obtained by only textbooks, you do not have "practical" and "collaborating" experience like experiments or lab works in university level. (My case, after recieving the degree, for this reason I enrolled to an undergraduate program at HGU, which operates the academic system like a liberal arts college and has amazing diversity among students. I think this was a really good decision for me :))

If you are a person who already have such a lot of field experience but still wanna get the degree, this might be a good option though.

My case was expanding my major! But if you already have a concrete interest and know the true motivation, and really wanna "check" if it is for you, this might be helpful to you. My friend who studied computer science through BDES, also majored in CS for his another bachelor’s degree in university and graduated in two years. He recently earned his master’s in AI as well.
