Most of my friends and advisors asked me how I earned a bachelor’s degree in two years during high school, so I wrote up how I did it!

> TL;DR:
> * I took the GED when I was in Grade 10.
> * The Korean government offers a degree program that can be completed *theoretically* in one year by taking a series of exams, and this degree is equivalent to a 4-year undergraduate education.
> * I passed it in two years.
> * Do I recommend this to everyone? Well, it depends on who you are. Efficiency is not everything.

Before I begin, you should know why it was possible for me to do this during high school:
**My secondary school was not accredited by the Korean government!**

It was a private boarding school that followed Christian and holistic education with an internationally oriented curriculum, which means this school highlighted freedom of speech and leadership training, so it chose not to be accredited by the Korean government. However, that doesn’t mean their education was somehow strange; this school is an IB school now, but it is still not accredited by Korean government. And many of my classmates went to universities worldwide. (I really love my school!)

Anyway, that was **my chance to take the GED** while attending high school because my school was not part of the Korean government's accreditation system. So when I was 15, I took the GED, which means I was qualified for the next step on paper!

---

And then, my teacher came up to me and said, “As you already got the GED, there is a way to get a bachelor’s degree in one year!!!”

*Whhaaaaaaatttttt😮*

### How is that even possible???

The program is called "Bachelor's Degree Examination for Self-Education System" or **BDES** (독학학위제 in Korean).

You don't have to be Korean to take this exam, but you have to be equivalent to a high school graduate! (btw, this exam is all in Korean)

It was originally made for people who could not afford higher education. It consists of four levels of examinations. One exam in a quarter: 
* On February, it's LIBERAL-ARTS EXAM. You should pass 5 general elective subjects that are relevant to your major. 
* On May, MAJOR-BASIC EXAM round! Now you should pass 6 fundamental major subjects, equivalent to sophomore year in university. 
* On August, MAJOR-ADVANCED EXAM round! You should pass 6 advanced major subjects, equivalent to junior year in university. 
* On October, the final boss, COMPREHENSIVE EXAM! To take the last exam, you must have passed all the earlier exams (or hold equivalent credits). This round is equivalent to the senior year in university. You should take 2 gen ed subjects and 4 advanced major subjects. And this round's score will decide your entire GPA! (and you can't retake once you pass the exam.)

As far as I know, there are similar programs in other countries as well. 
* **China**: STHEE (The Self-Taught Higher Education Examination, 高等教育自学考试)
* **USA**: CLEP (College-Level Examination Program) 


### And a **warning sign** here...
This exam is SUPER hard. 

Not because I took them while attending classes in high school, but because I had to study without any guidance. The only thing they offer is a table of contents. So only thing you have to do is memorize literally "everything" in the textbook and listen to every source on MOOC or K-MOOC or such public higher education sources via the internet about the 5-6 subjects in 3 months. When you first encounter the textbook, you have no idea what is important or not... and having no peers to study with is a minus. 
It is a frustrating and anxious process as you only have one chance to take the exam each year and you have no one guiding you! You have to memorize and make every piece of knowledge that requires one year at college in a quarter, you have to study persistently and know YOUR learning style! You can’t cooperate with others but only with yourself. You should trust that consistency makes you better day by day. 

---

Yet, as much as you invest your time and effort, you will earn it in the end. It might take more than 4 years if you couldn't pass all as you should retake in the next year if you fail in the exam; however, it is efficient in time and cost compared to the normal university system.

I would not recommend this path to everyone to be honest. It is true that BDES seems very "productive;" however, it's super lonely and stressful. But if you are super passionate about something and have a concrete goal, it might be a good option for you. 

But by going through such processes, I earned:
* **How to acquire new knowledge and make it my own from scratch**. For me, I read the textbook 3 times and made my own notes! (I'll share my note later!)
* **What my true interest is**. In my case, I started psychology due to curiosity about the mechanisms of unconsciousness and abnormality, and I realized my true passion comes from curiosity about human biology! (Thanks to the exam on cognitive neuroscience!) 
* And of course, **a degree**. Since this system is not familiar to many people, I end up explaining how I earned it at 18 every time I meet someone new, which can be a bit of a hassle. (Hence this post!)

### Do I recommend this path?

The best thing is that I saved time by just diving into the studies that I was interested in. If I didn’t study psychology at that time, I think I would never have known I actually loved discovering the mechanisms of disease and creating a new method, not just observing behavior.

Even though you got a bachelor's degree through this program, I don't think it makes you ready enough for graduate studies right after the degree conferral. Because that knowledge is obtained only from textbooks, you do not have "practical" and "collaborating" experience like experiments or lab work at the university level. (In my case, after receiving the degree, for this reason I enrolled in an undergraduate program at HGU, which operates the academic system like a liberal arts college and has amazing diversity among students. I think this was a really good decision for me :))

If you are a person who already has a lot of field experience but still want to get the degree, this might be a good option though. I might also recommend it to those who already have a bachelor's degree but want to study a new field and are not yet ready for graduate studies (such as an art graduate who wants to try psychology). 

If you already have a concrete interest and know the true motivation, and really want to "check" if it is for you, this might be helpful to you. In my case, I expanded my major from psychology to biology and AI, my friend who studied computer science through BDES, also majored in CS for his second bachelor’s degree in university and graduated in two years. He recently earned his master’s in AI as well.
